## Problem Statement

revoy2 is a clean-room coding harness that treats autonomous work as a governed
search tree. One month old: 2,276 tracked files, 14 `packages/arbor-*`, 148 ADRs <!-- sur-05:historical -->
as of the 2026-08-31 integration follow-up, a gate battery of 56 declared gates, <!-- sur-05:historical -->
38 classed stop-tier, 36 actually executed in
the stop runs observed this session, hash-chained evidence stores, a
decision-interview ledger, and
a visual-intelligence graph that detects unreachable symbols, hollow candidates,
and declaration-to-implementation gaps.

The thesis under it: the bottleneck in agentic coding is trusting work you did not
watch happen, and the remedy is enforced, mechanized governance rather than
after-the-fact observation. The governance is designed to be imperceptible — the
agent authors the ADR, the operator sees narrow decision prompts.

The gap: after a month, the only workload the system has been proven against is
building itself.

## Demand Evidence

None yet, stated plainly by the founder: *"Only me so far — nobody outside has
run it."* Pre-product. No outside user has run it on their own repository.

The pain is nonetheless concretely named rather than hypothetical — the failure
modes are specific and observed: *"stubbing, code drift, partial implementation,
the nature of cut-off training dates"* across *"finite context windows and

One piece of live evidence for the thesis, captured during the session: the stop
battery blocked with `gate:obligations` red because ADR-150 carried three
obligations whose proof named `packages/arbor-mcp/tests/skills/bundle.test.ts`,
which did not yet exist. The gate caught a partial implementation against an
agent with no intention of writing that test. That is the thesis working on real
input, on one repo, uncounted.

Follow-up on 2026-08-31: the blocked state was discharged rather than waived.
Bundle support and the cited proof test landed, the operator's later acceptance
was recorded, and ADR-150 is now Accepted. The incident remains evidence of the
gate detecting partial implementation; it is no longer an accurate description
of current repository debt.

A second instance, observed during the session itself and noted by the founder at
the time. This session was structurally the same mechanism: a governed interview
that surfaced each claim as a narrow decision point rather than letting it pass
implicitly. It caught two confidently-asserted and wrong architectural claims from
an agent — that ADR authoring imposed operator burden, and that the governance
compiler was deferred to 1.0.0 — and the corrections happened only because the
structure forced the agent to go read `greenfield-command.test.ts` and ADR-143
instead of continuing to reason from a README.

The asymmetry is worth recording: here the interviewee was the agent and the
corrections came from the operator; in revoy2 the interviewee is the operator and
the claims under test are the agent's. Same mechanism, opposite seat, and it works
in both directions for the same reason — a decision surfaced as a narrow question
gets answered, and a decision left implicit gets assumed. This is anecdote, not
measurement, which is exactly what Success Criterion 3 exists to fix.

## Status Quo

Two named populations, no named individual in either.

1. Non-technical builders shipping consequential tools with powerful models and
   no ability to audit the result. Their status quo is reading a diff they cannot
   evaluate, or not reading it.
2. Organizations that cannot tolerate rogue agent actions, benign or otherwise,
   and that value auditability and stability. Their status quo is CI, human
   review, and revert.

**RETRACTED CLAIM — read this before citing the regulation anywhere.** An earlier
draft of this document asserted that EU AI Act Article 12 high-risk obligations
"reached full enforcement on 2026-08-02", that the article requires a queryable
record of AI-driven decisions "explicitly including whether each governance
intervention was a hard gate or a soft gate", and that revoy2 had converged on the
regulation. That paragraph was built from a search-result summary and was never
checked against the source. Checking it against the source:

- Article 12's actual requirement is narrow and technical: *"High-risk AI systems
  shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the
  lifetime of the system."* It says nothing about hard versus soft gates, and
  nothing about governance interventions. That phrasing was a commentator's gloss.
- The dates do not support "28 days ago". The primary reference gives Annex III
  systems 2027-12-02 and Annex I systems 2028-08-02. 2026-08-02 is a real
  milestone in the original timeline and there has been movement on delays that
  this session could not resolve. Status: **contested**, not settled.
- Applicability was never established. Annex III's high-risk categories are
  biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services,
  law enforcement, migration, and justice. A coding harness is not obviously any
  of them, so the regulation may impose no obligation at all on revoy2's users.

**What this costs the plan.** The regulatory deadline was one of two arguments for
prioritising the attestation work and for taking the compliance buyer seriously.
It is withdrawn. The remaining argument for C is the moat argument — accumulated
evidence that is expensive to abandon — which stands on its own and does not need
a deadline. Do not re-introduce the regulatory framing without reading the
Official Journal text and establishing that a coding harness falls in scope.

**What survives, stated at the strength it has earned.** Enterprise interest in
agent auditability is real and observable in the 2026 discourse (Uber's three
governance layers before scaling agent adoption is a concrete example). That is
market weather, not a compliance clock, and it justifies attention rather than
urgency.

## Constraints

- Solo founder, pre-revenue, one month in.
- Private repo on GitHub free plan; branch protection returns 403, so the
  strength ladder's BLOCKED tier is unreachable (ADR-025). Enforcement is
  local-only and `--no-verify` bypasses it.
- Governance layer is already **97 of 117 gate/governance scripts free of any
  `@arbor/*` reference**, and demonstrably runs inside Claude Code sessions via
  `.claude/skills/` and `scripts/hooks/gate-stop.sh` using none of the fourteen
  packages. Pinned and reproducible, since Approach A's estimate rests on it:
  `ls scripts/*.mjs scripts/*.ts | wc -l` → 117;
  `grep -rlE '@arbor/' scripts/*.mjs scripts/*.ts | wc -l` → 20; 117 − 20 = 97.
  A different glob gives a different number: `.mjs` + `.sh` yields 112/98. The
  boundary must be pinned to the exact command before A is scoped, because
  "gate/governance script" is not otherwise a countable category.
- Every count in this document is pinned to the command that produced it, because
  these numbers drift fast here and an earlier draft misattributed one of them:
  `jq '.gates | length' scripts/gate-manifest.json` → 56 declared;
  `grep -c '"cost_class": "stop"' scripts/execution-profile.json` → 38 stop-classed;
  `node scripts/gate-all.mjs --list --tier stop | wc -l` → 36 executed;
  `bun scripts/vi-findings.mjs` → `gap-implementation-to-intent: 1014 record(s)`
  and `violations: 8`. Note the 1,014 is a record count inside a single
  `findings.jsonl` line, not a line count; the file holds 58 lines total.
  For contrast, `scripts/hooks/gate-stop.sh`'s own header still reads
  "Measured: 17 gates, ~3.7s wall" — stale against the current 36. Counts in this
  repo require a command and a date, never a remembered figure.
- Language coverage is real but narrow: `scripts/gate-manifest.json` declares 12
  language-scoped gates, all TypeScript, plus one JavaScript. No rule-packs
  directory exists. A Python repo today receives `unsupported` across all of them.
- ADR-143's LSP inversion is **staged, not flipped** — `vi-extract-ts` remains the
  primary static-fact producer while the LSP producer runs alongside it, and every
  disagreement is a finding to resolve before the flip.
- Two agent processes share this working tree; broad `git add` is unsafe.

## Premises

Accepted:

- **P1' (revised after cross-model challenge).** The governance layer is the
  valuable half. It is **not yet defensible** — the detectors are reimplementable
  and a departing user loses nothing, because their evidence lives in their own
  repo. Defensibility must be designed, not assumed.
- **P2.** The harness half competes on a commodity layer against well-funded
  teams at near-zero switching cost.

Contested, left unticked by the founder, and deliberately **not built on**:

- P3: portability is the binding constraint. (Rejected while still choosing the
  portability proof as the wedge — compatible if portability is the cheapest
  *test* of a constraint lying elsewhere.)
- P4: the two customers pull in opposite directions (invisible vs attestable).

Unmeasured, and it is the founder's own MVP claim: *"the MVP is merely a PoC that
the gating helps more than hinders."* Falsifiable, central, and after a month
there is no score for it. Every gate run emits a verdict; nobody is counting.

## Corrections Made During the Session

Recorded because the corrected version is what the plan rests on.

- ADR authorship is fully mechanized (`scripts/record-interview.mjs`,
  `decision-queue.mjs`); the interview skill's first rule is *"Ask. Do not
  decide."* The operator surface is narrow multiple-choice prompts. An earlier
  claim that governance imposes authoring burden on the human was wrong.
- **ADR-143 (accepted 2026-08-27) already is the governance compiler**: gates
  declare their languages, repos declare theirs, `unsupported` is a distinct third
  verdict that fails the battery at stop tier, and `gate:language-census` flags
  undeclared languages. An earlier claim that the differentiated layer was
  deferred to 1.0.0 was wrong.
- Greenfield runs against real external repos exist:
  `packages/arbor-mcp/tests/greenfield-command.test.ts` drives the real CLI `main`
  against throwaway `git init` repos per ADR-114. Narrowing that survives: the
  runtime is `confirmableStubRuntime`, so what is proven is preflight and
  isolation, not the battery on foreign ground; and a freshly created repo is the
  easy case, having no legacy shape to reconcile.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Extract the agnostic core — `revoy-guard` (CHOSEN, first)

Pull the 97-of-117 gates that never touch `@arbor/*` into a standalone CLI that
installs on any repo. Run it against three real outside repositories and emit a
per-commit attestation.

- Effort: M (human ~5 days / CC ~2 sessions), of which roughly the first day is
  deciding and implementing the extraction seam below, not writing gate code.
  That item is flagged "expensive to reverse" and "not yet decided", so treat the
  5 days as contingent on the seam decision landing early rather than mid-build.
  Risk: Low.
- Reuses: `scripts/*.mjs`, `scripts/execution-profile.json`, `scripts/gate-manifest.json`.
- Cost: two codebases to keep honest unless the extraction is a real seam; the
  agnostic gates are the least differentiated ones in the set.

### Approach B: Finish the compiler — stock the packs

Complete ADR-143. Flip LSP to primary so the seven graph-derived gates become
portable without porting, then ship one non-TypeScript rule pack and prove the
census/`unsupported` path on a real polyglot repo.

- Effort: XL (human ~4-6 weeks / CC ~2-3 weeks). Risk: Med-High.
- Not chosen now: longest path to outside evidence when there is none, and the
  staged LSP reconciliation has no natural end date by its own design. This is
  month two and three.

### Approach C: Ship the artifact, not the checker (CHOSEN, second)

Make the attestation the product. Per-commit signed record: repo fingerprint,
gates selected and why, hard/soft/unsupported status, command and output hashes,
overrides, verdict. Chain it so history compounds and departure costs the user
their evidence.

- Effort: M (human ~1-2 weeks / CC ~3-4 sessions). Risk: Med.
- Reuses: hash-chained ledgers, `decisions/interviews.jsonl`, evidence stores,
  the obligations mechanism.
- Cost: an attestation nobody has agreed to read is a file, not a standard.

## Recommended Approach

**C-schema, then A, then C-ledger.**

Corrected from a flat "A, then C" after review found it contradicted the
Dependencies section. The attestation *schema* must be designed before A ships so
A's runs emit the chained format natively rather than needing regeneration; the
full *ledger* — chaining, accumulation, the properties that make departure costly
— is built after A has produced real runs to chain. C is therefore split, not
sequenced wholesale after A.

A buys the falsification chosen as the wedge within a week, from code whose
harness-independence is already a measured fact rather than a hope. C is what
converts that evidence into the moat P1' says does not yet exist — it is the
direct answer to Codex's challenge. The retracted regulatory deadline supplies
no urgency or applicability claim for this sequence.

B is right and is what months two and three are for, once something outside this
machine has run.

## Success Criteria

1. `revoy-guard` runs to completion on **three repositories not written by the
   founder**, at least one of which is not primarily TypeScript.
2. For each run, the number of gates that return `PASS`, `FAIL`, and
   `unsupported` is recorded. The `unsupported` count on the non-TypeScript repo
   is the honest measure of current portability.
3. **The help-vs-hinder score exists.** One JSONL row per gate invocation at
   `.revoy/help-hinder.jsonl`, so it is diffable and appendable rather than
   reconstructed from memory:
   `{ts, repo, gate_id, verdict: pass|fail|unsupported, blocked: bool,
   block_correct: bool|null, minutes_to_resolve: number|null, note}`.
   `block_correct` is the operator's judgement and may be null while undecided;
   a null is data, not a gap. This settles the founder's own MVP thesis and
   currently has no instrument.
4. A `.revoy/attestations/<commit>.json` is produced that a third party can read
   without access to revoy2, and that names hard vs soft vs unsupported per gate.
5. At least one finding on an outside repo that the repo's own CI did not catch.
   This is the only criterion that speaks to value rather than portability.

   **Null-result path, stated in advance so it is not rationalised later.** The
   extracted set is by A's own Cost line the least differentiated portion of the
   battery, so a null result here is a plausible outcome of construction rather
   than of execution failure. If all three repos return nothing CI missed, that
   does not kill the thesis — it says the *agnostic* gates are not where the value
   is, and it promotes Approach B (stock the rule packs, where the differentiated
   detectors live) ahead of C. Decide that at the Assignment's end, not during it.

6. **P1' is testable and currently untested by criteria 1-5.** Time-box it: hand a
   competent engineer only the documented behaviour of two extracted detectors and
   measure how long equivalent detection takes them. Under two weeks confirms
   Codex's challenge and makes the C-ledger urgent rather than optional. This is
   the only criterion that measures defensibility rather than function.

   **This criterion is not free and nothing else in this document accounts for
   it.** It needs a person who is not the founder, which for a solo pre-revenue
   project means sourcing, paying or trading for their time, and deciding what
   they may see. Cheapest honest substitute if that is out of reach: publish the
   two detectors' documented behaviour and treat *any* third-party
   reimplementation appearing within the window as the same signal. Do not quietly
   drop the criterion because it is inconvenient; it is the only test of the
   premise this document opens with.

**Which criteria The Assignment actually discharges.** The Assignment is manual
and pre-extraction, so it closes **2, 3, and 5** and partially informs 1. It does
**not** close 1 (needs `revoy-guard` to exist and run), 4 (needs a real
`.revoy/attestations/<commit>.json`), or 6 (needs a third party). Those three
belong to A and C, which are recommended but not authorized. Finishing the
Assignment is not finishing this document.

## Distribution Plan

- `npx revoy-guard init` / `npx revoy-guard check --base origin/main`, published
  to npm. Matches archgate's distribution shape, which is the observable reason it
  has users.
- `init` writes a profile plus a CI workflow; the checker exits non-zero on a
  hard-gate failure so CI blocks the merge.
- Publish from GitHub Actions on tag. Note the existing constraint: this repo's
  own branch protection is unavailable on the free plan (ADR-025), so the
  published tool can offer CI enforcement that the harness repo itself cannot
  currently enforce on its own pushes.
- Licensing: revoy2 is currently MIT and archgate is Apache-2.0. Treat the
  extracted tool's licence as undecided (see Open Questions) rather than inherited
  — publishing is the point at which the choice becomes irreversible.

## Dependencies

- Extraction seam must be real, not a copy. If the 97 scripts are duplicated
  rather than shared, both copies rot. Mechanism to decide before writing any
  code, since the choice is expensive to reverse: a Bun/npm workspace package
  that revoy2 consumes as a dependency is the default recommendation (one source,
  revoy2 becomes the first consumer and therefore the first integration test);
  git subtree and published-package-only are the alternatives. Not yet decided.
- Attestation schema (C) should be designed before A ships, so A's runs already
  emit the chained format rather than needing regeneration.
- `gate:vi-findings` triage is an operator-only verdict by design and cannot be
  automated. On an outside repo, triage volume is the scaling risk: 1,014 records
  under `gap-implementation-to-intent` on this repo alone, and 8 findings await a
  verdict right now.

## Open Questions
